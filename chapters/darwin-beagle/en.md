How does a voyage become scientific knowledge? Follow Charles Darwin on the second HMS Beagle voyage, from 1831 to 1836, and explore his shore work in Galápagos.

## Surveying, collecting, comparing

Robert FitzRoy commands the survey ship. Darwin collects and observes, supported by crew members and assistants such as Syms Covington. Participation does not mean that everyone travelled together at every moment.

## A closer look at Galápagos

The Beagle reaches the islands on 15 September 1835. Darwin first lands on San Cristóbal on 16 September, followed by visits to Floreana, Isabela and Santiago. He stays on Santiago from 8 to 17 October while the ship collects water elsewhere. Open these events and choose “Show on the map”.

## No finished theory on one island

The observations were studied further after the voyage. Differences between mockingbirds on different islands were especially significant. John Gould recognised the relationship of the finches after the return. This is a story of observation and later interpretation, not a single moment when a complete theory of evolution appeared.
