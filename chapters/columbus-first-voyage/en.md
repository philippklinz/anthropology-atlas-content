Open “Santa María in 3D” under “Discover”. Rotate the ship and zoom in to explore its wooden hull, deck, sails and rigging. The model depicts the ship on the voyage of 1492. ## A western route to Asia

Columbus seeks a sea route to Asia, but reaches Caribbean islands. “Discovery of America” expresses a European perspective: the inhabitants of Guanahani and the other islands already lived there. Their societies are not an empty backdrop.

## Encounter and claims to power

The expedition meets Lucayan inhabitants on Guanahani. Columbus claims land for his sponsors. The account also records the forcible taking of islanders. After the loss of the Santa María, Guacanagarí’s people help salvage it; the stay produces the outpost La Navidad. These encounters form part of the beginnings of European colonisation.

## Which Guanahani?

The landing on 12 October 1492 is recorded, but identification with a modern island is disputed. Our point is only a placeholder in the Bahamas region. It does not establish any candidate location.

## Reading the account critically

Columbus’s original log is lost. We use a later translation of the account transmitted through Bartolomé de las Casas. Claims about islanders’ intentions therefore are not an independent Indigenous voice. The route is schematic; the return follows selected Niña stations. Original Julian dates remain visible.
