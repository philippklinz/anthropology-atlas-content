# Ugarit Around 1200 BCE

Three sites on the coast of present-day Syria reveal different aspects of a connected world. A carved lid takes us to the harbour at Minet el-Beida. At Ugarit a king reports attacks. Farther south, at Tell Tweini, excavators found houses and storerooms that were built after a fire. Each stop shows what a particular find can tell us about changes around 1200 BCE.

## Minet el-Beida

The harbour of Mahadu lay in a bay just under two kilometres from the mound of Ugarit. In 1929, excavators found a lid made of elephant ivory in Tomb III at the harbour site. It once belonged to a small cylindrical box. In the photograph, a figure holds branches towards two upright goats. The Louvre dates the lid to approximately **1300–1150 BCE**.

The catalogue describes the scene as Mycenaean-inspired. Forms and motifs could travel with objects or be adopted by workshops. Where this lid was carved and how it reached the tomb remain open questions. On the map, the harbour and Ugarit are close together. The broad date comes from the museum catalogue for the lid. The lid offers little evidence for when ships called at the bay or when the harbour was abandoned.

## Ras Shamra

Excavators at Ugarit recovered clay tablet **RS 20.238**. In Akkadian cuneiform, the king of Ugarit addresses the king of Alašiya, associated with Cyprus. He reports attacks on settlements in his kingdom and says his own ships are elsewhere. The text leaves the sender and recipient unnamed.

The excavators first thought the tablet had still been in a firing kiln in the palace. A later interpretation identifies the installation as an oven inserted after the destruction. The letter and other tablets may have fallen from an upper floor. An earlier date for its composition is possible. Whether the message reached Cyprus remains unclear.

Another part of the mound yielded tablet fragment **AO 19992**, which records a sequence of letters in Ugaritic cuneiform. Wedge-shaped signs impressed in clay could serve different writing systems. At one site, the two tablets preserve an alphabet sequence and a message from one royal court to another.

## Tell Tweini

South of Ugarit, excavators in Field A uncovered older floors, burned debris and later stone walls. In the lower photograph, the darker layer lies between the floor and the wall. The labels in the photograph show how the excavators interpreted the layer in connection with the so-called Sea Peoples. The layers show that the fire and later construction took place at different times.

The team dates new houses built on the earlier remains to the **twelfth century BCE**. In places, people reused earlier floors and walls. From a later phase, large jars survived against the wall of a storeroom. Seeds from the jars helped the team place the end of this phase at about **1050–1000 BCE**. A newer model allows for a later date as well.

Move between Ugarit and Tell Tweini on the map. A letter survives at one site. At the other, later walls, jars and seeds show how people built again and kept supplies after a fire.
