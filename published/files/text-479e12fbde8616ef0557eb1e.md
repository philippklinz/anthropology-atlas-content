# Brunhild, Fredegund and the treaty of Andelot

What did a queen actually receive when someone gave her a city? In November 587 a king, his nephew and the young man's mother met at Andelot. Among the clauses pledging their loyalty to each other is one about Cahors, far to the south-west. From that day Brunhild was to hold the city together with its lands and the people who lived there. We know this from a copy of the agreement that Bishop Gregory of Tours included in his Histories. How had a woman's claim to a city come to the negotiating table?

## A family divided between towns {#brunhild}

When Chlothar I died in 561, his four sons divided the kingdom. Three of the brothers figure in this story. Sigibert married Brunhild, a daughter of the Visigothic king Athanagild. Their son was called Childebert. Sigibert's brother Chilperic married Brunhild's sister Galswinth. When Galswinth died, claims to the property she had received at her marriage remained unsettled. Chilperic's next wife was Fredegund. A third brother, Guntram, outlived the other two and had to negotiate with both their widows and their children.

Gregory shows what it meant for a child when the throne changed hands. When Sigibert was murdered at Vitry in 575, Brunhild and the children were in Paris. Duke Gundovald secretly carried little Childebert away. At Christmas he was acclaimed king. His mother fell into Chilperic's hands. He sent her to Rouen and had her daughters detained at Meaux. A mother, a son and his sisters had shared a household. Now their separation determined who could reach the new king.

## Who could offer a widow protection? {#fredegund}

Nine years later it was Chilperic's court that lost its king. After his murder in 584 Fredegund sought shelter with Bishop Ragnemod in the cathedral at Paris. Gregory even distinguishes two portions of her treasure. The part stored within the city walls was available to her there. Officials took other pieces from Chelles, including a large gold dish, to Childebert at Meaux. Fredegund appealed to her brother-in-law Guntram. Her own son, the future Chlothar II, was still an infant.

Gregory reports grave allegations against Fredegund. He also tells us that a bishop received her and that her envoys could obtain an audience with Guntram. The historian Gregory Halfond has identified seventeen bishops who supported the royal couple Chilperic and Fredegund. These connections help explain whose door a woman could knock on after her husband died. An account confined to her enemies would miss precisely this aspect of her power.

## Andelot and Cahors on the same map {#andelot-and-cahors}

In 587 Brunhild took part in the negotiations herself. The opening of the treaty names her alongside Guntram and her son Childebert II. Bishops and magnates acted as intermediaries. Several cities were reckoned in shares. The third of Paris that once belonged to Sigibert was to stay with Guntram. A city's territory and the people who lived on it came into the reckoning. It would be difficult to translate this arrangement into a line cutting across the streets of Paris today.

Cahors presented a different problem. It had belonged to the gifts Galswinth received at her marriage. Brunhild was to take possession of Cahors at once. Guntram would keep four other cities for his lifetime. On his death they were to pass to Brunhild and her heirs. Look at the two places highlighted on the map for 587. Andelot was where the parties met. Cahors was a subject of their agreement. Whether a family on the River Lot felt the change when its next payment fell due is beyond what the treaty can tell us. Three months before the November agreement, the community at Poitiers had lost its founder. ## Who would take care of the children? {#chalon}

Guntram promised to protect Childebert's sons if their father died before him. The text also names Childebert's mother Brunhild, his sister Chlodosind and his wife Faileuba individually. Their powers to dispose of lands, revenues and gifts were to be secure. A separate clause guaranteed Guntram's own daughter Clotild a continued right to whatever her father gave her.

Further on, the treaty allowed the men of both kings to cross into the other's realm on private business or on their lord's behalf. Enticing away the other king's followers remained forbidden. A traveller could therefore have reason to explain whom he served. Gregory tells how Guntram had the agreement read aloud again during an embassy to Chalon-sur-Saône in 588. Even Fredegund's envoys became a matter of dispute on that occasion. The oath at Andelot gave those involved something they could invoke the next time they quarrelled.
