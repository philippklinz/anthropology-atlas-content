# Four brothers and a third of Paris

His father had barely been buried when Chilperic took the treasure from a royal estate. He used it to buy the support of men he hoped to count on. Then he entered Paris. That is how Gregory of Tours tells the story for 561. In the process Chilperic had passed over three brothers. They joined forces and drove him out. When the four men came to terms, his share was Soissons. How had Paris come to be available in the first place? The story goes back a generation to their grandfather’s death.

## What did Clovis’s sons inherit? {#succession-511}

In 511 Clovis was buried in Paris in the church of the Holy Apostles, which he and his wife Clotild had built. Gregory follows the king’s death with a brief statement. Theuderic, Chlodomer, Childebert and Chlothar divided their father’s kingdom among them in equal shares. What counted as equal? The bishop gives neither the brothers’ revenues nor a place where they negotiated. The marker at Paris therefore belongs to the burial. Earlier that year, farther south, [bishops had gathered at Clovis’s summons in Orléans](chapter:clovis-and-his-bishops#orleans).

## A helmet from the peat bog at Vézeronce {#vezeronce-524}

Thirteen years later Chlodomer fought Godomar, king of the Burgundians, near Vézeronce in the territory of Vienne. Gregory tells how Chlodomer outstripped his men in the pursuit. Enemies imitated his followers’ rallying cry. He rode towards them and was killed. His mother Clotild took in his three young sons. Two were later killed at their own uncles’ instigation. The third, Chlodoald, had his hair cut and became a cleric. Their father’s death had consequences for the children long before the next generation could divide an inheritance.

In 1870 a helmet came to light in a peat bog at Vézeronce. Six metal bands, fastened with rivets, hold the plates of the bowl together. Traces of gilding remain. So costly an object prompted an obvious question. Could Chlodomer have worn it? Its owner’s name is unknown. Nor does the find pinpoint the spot where the king fell. A drawing in the 1909 Grenoble museum catalogue shows the bands and rows of rivets. The plate is linked in the sources under Le Musée de Grenoble.

## Autun and the end of a kingship {#burgundy-534}

Godomar recovered his kingdom after Chlodomer’s death. It took another campaign to end the rule of the Burgundian kings. Gregory names a place. Childebert and Chlothar besieged Autun and Godomar fled. The chronicler Marius of Avenches records the division of the conquered kingdom under the year conventionally reckoned as 534. Just when the besiegers arrived outside Autun remains uncertain. Theuderic’s son Theudebert also received a share of the conquest. Readers who come across Burgundy in later Frankish sources will recognise the old name, now used under a different royal family.

## Four seats and a third of Paris {#four-seats-561}

When Childebert died in 558, his brother Chlothar gained the whole kingdom. Three years later Chlothar’s four sons buried their father at Soissons. Chilperic’s bid for Paris now makes sense. His uncle Childebert had held a royal seat there. In this second division Gregory matches each brother with a predecessor’s seat. Charibert received Childebert’s Paris, Guntram Chlodomer’s Orléans, Chilperic his father’s seat at Soissons and Sigibert Theuderic’s Reims. Readers could name these four places even if the boundaries of the shares were unknown to them. The list also leaves open how long any of the kings stayed there. The historian Marc Widdowson reads its remarkably tidy pairings in light of the later struggles over inheritance that Gregory himself witnessed.

A document from 587 gives a closer view of what went with a city. A *civitas* included its surrounding district. The Treaty of Andelot expressly allowed Guntram to keep a third of Paris which had once belonged to Sigibert. Anyone looking for a line through the streets of Paris would learn more from the provisions concerning people, possessions and income. [The negotiators at Andelot also settled what Queen Brunhild should inherit at Cahors.](chapter:merovingian-queens-and-the-treaty#andelot-and-cahors)

## Where could a judge come from? {#paris-edict-614}

Gregory occasionally uses the name Austrasia for the eastern lands. Neustria, later the usual name for the western kingdom, is absent from his works. By the seventh century Austrasia, Neustria and Burgundy were familiar terms for political regions. The Chronicle of Fredegar tells how leading men in Austrasia and Burgundy withdrew their support from Brunhild in 613. Chilperic’s son Chlothar II had her killed and became sole ruler.

In October 614 he issued an edict in Paris. One provision required judges to come from the area where they served. A judge who had wrongfully taken something from someone was to make good the loss out of his own property. Even with a single king, it could matter a great deal to the injured party where the judge called home.
