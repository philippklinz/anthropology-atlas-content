# Alaric, Rome under siege and a home in Aquitaine

How could someone put pressure on a Roman emperor's court by besieging a different city? Look at Italy. Ravenna lies on the Adriatic and Rome farther south on the Tiber. Emperor Honorius resided at Ravenna. At Rome, senators could raise considerable sums of money. Hundreds of thousands of inhabitants depended on food coming into the city from its port. Alaric and his followers could interrupt those supplies, with consequences for both cities.

## An army between courts

Around 395 Alaric emerged as the leader of Gothic troops in the Balkans. His following changed over time. It included Tervingi and Greuthungi. They were later joined by survivors of the army with which Radagaisus had been defeated in Italy in 406. The story of these people coming together belongs to the early history of the Visigoths. We can trace the group in accounts of campaigns, military service in the empire and negotiations. The movements of individual families are much harder to recover.

## Rome and Rimini

The later historian Zosimus describes the siege of 408. Alaric, he writes, cut off deliveries from the port along the Tiber. Food rations in Rome shrank. Two women with ties to the imperial family, Laeta and her mother Pissamena, reportedly distributed food from their own allowance. The senate eventually raised a ransom. Both details matter. City dwellers had to find something to eat on the same day their representatives were negotiating over gold and silver.

In 409 the Roman negotiator Jovius met Alaric at Ariminum, modern Rimini. Zosimus lists grain, gold and a place to settle among the demands. Any agreement needed the approval of the emperor at Ravenna. For those negotiating with Alaric, and for the people he had to feed, waiting meant something different from what it meant at court behind secure walls. No lasting agreement was reached in Alaric's lifetime.

## Rome in August 410

On 24 August 410, Alaric's followers entered and sacked Rome. Two years later Jerome wrote to Principia about the fate of her companion Marcella. Soldiers, he related, had sought gold in Marcella's home and beaten her. Eventually they escorted both women to the basilica of the apostle Paul. Jerome mentioned people who had told him the story. He was far from Rome during the sack. His letter commemorates one particular woman. In The City of God, Augustine told of people who found shelter in churches. He wanted his readers to understand what the disaster meant for their faith. The two texts bring different experiences into view.

## Toulouse and Aquitaine

Alaric died in southern Italy later in 410. It was under his successors that Gothic groups secured a settlement in south-western Gaul. In 418 the Roman commander Constantius recalled them from Hispania. The accounts name the province of Aquitanica Secunda and neighbouring cities. Toulouse became a centre for Gothic kings. Eight years, changes of leadership and further fighting separate the entry into Rome from this settlement. Scholars still debate exactly which places the agreement covered and how densely the newcomers settled. The chapter [from Toulouse to the Council of Toledo](chapter:visigoths-toulouse-to-toledo) traces the shift of Visigothic centres of power towards Hispania.
