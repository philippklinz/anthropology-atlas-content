# Living at the Edge of the Ice

On the Don River, archaeologists find a ring of mammoth bones. At the Sea of Galilee, the floors of brush huts survive. In present-day Jordan, sediments show that people gathered beside a wetland that lasted through part of the site's occupation. The three places reveal different ways people lived during the last glacial period.

Most great ice sheets were near their maximum extent between about 26,500 and 19,000–20,000 years ago. Researchers call this phase the **Last Glacial Maximum**. The time range brings together many regional histories. The chapter map locates three sites on a present-day basemap.

## Kostenki

Kostenki 11 lies on the Don River in present-day Russia. The circle studied here is about 12.5 metres across. Five consistent radiocarbon dates give a calibrated range of 25,063 to 24,490 BP. BP counts calendar years before 1950.

Charcoal and burned bones survived inside the circle. People had wood as fuel alongside bone. Some of the charcoal came from conifers. The charcoal alone tells us little about how densely trees grew in the surroundings.

Did people live inside the circle? A roof spanning that width would have been a demanding structure. The researchers found relatively little of the small stone debris they would expect from long-term habitation. They discuss other possibilities, including storage. The remains leave the shape and use of the structure open.

## Ohalo

Farther south, Ohalo II lies beside the Sea of Galilee, in present-day Israel. The excavation identified six hut features with shallow sunken floors. Especially well-preserved remains in Brush Hut 1 reveal which plants people used to build. Smaller branches, leaves and grasses covered thicker branches. Some floors were renewed several times.

Dark shapes mark huts on the excavation plan and paler marks represent hearths. Look at the scale on the right. Five metres give a sense of how large these spaces were. Dates between about 23,500 and 22,500 BP cover repeated stays beside the lake. The plan alone cannot tell us which huts were in use at the same time.

You will find this same place in **A Day of Making a Living**. The Ohalo II finds help us think about shelter as well as food.

## Kharaneh

Kharaneh IV lies in present-day eastern Jordan. Sediments suggest a local wetland during part of the site's use. The dense archaeological layers record repeated visits.

The floor of Structure 1 measures about 3.2 by 2.2 metres. Above it lies a charred layer that the excavators interpret as the burned upper part of the hut. Samples below and above the floor place the feature around 19,200–18,800 BP. The whole site covers a longer span.

These are three different kinds of building evidence. Large bones survived at Kostenki, plant materials at Ohalo II, and a floor with a charred layer above it at Kharaneh IV. What would be your first question at each place?
