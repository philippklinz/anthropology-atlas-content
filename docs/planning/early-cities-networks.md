# Early Cities, Trade and Writing

Status: research draft, 2026-11-23.

The discovery group **Erste Städte, Handel und Schrift / Early Cities, Trade and Writing** compares evidence from roughly 4600–1800 BCE. These limits describe this selection of evidence. Each chapter has its own viewing window. Dates, growth and the organisation of settlements differ by region. Some selected finds overlap the preceding postglacial chapters.

| Chapter | Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How Cities Grow | What can sherd scatters, walls and storerooms each tell us? | Tell Brak surface survey, Uruk Stone Cone Building, Arslantepe Period VIA complex |
| Marks, Seals and Writing | How do archaeologists distinguish a seal, a sign and a record? | Susa bulla, Uruk administrative tablet, Abydos tomb U-j labels |
| Copper, Gold and Tin | How do grave goods, metal chemistry and old accounts trace exchange? | Varna I Grave 43, Tisenjoch axe, tin caravan account associated with Kaneš |

| Building Together on the Andean Coast | What do early public structures and coastal food remains show? | Sechín Bajo circular plaza, Áspero food remains and fishing weight, Caral Great Pyramid |

## Editorial decisions

- Use one discovery period with region and topic facets. Cross-regional comparison is the purpose of this grouping. Local phase names and approximate dates belong on the relevant stations.
- Give each chapter three steps and independently dated evidence. Map points orient the reader at known sites. A line requires a sourced path. A settlement-area estimate does not by itself define a dated outline.
- Reuse Uruk and its orientation point between the city and writing packages. Each chapter includes the shared records it uses, while the urban package owns them. Separate the Uruk architectural evidence from the administrative tablet.
- Keep provenance at the level of the description in the source. Distinguish dated excavation context, approximate period assignment and inferred source region. Read material replication rights separately from source citations.
- Start comparisons from details a reader can see or locate. Explain what the archaeologists measured, what they infer and where another reading remains possible.
