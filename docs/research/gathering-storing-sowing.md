# Gathering, Storing and Sowing · research notes

The chapter compares storage architecture, cereal morphology and an archaeobotanical sequence in Southwest Asia. Its viewing interval is 11,700–9,500 years BP, referenced to 1950. Dates in the evidence notes are calibrated calendar years BP unless labelled as a laboratory radiocarbon determination. The three settings are displayed as independent findsites. Site orientation tolerances are editorial rather than statistical estimates.

| Dhra′, Jordan | 11,300–11,200 cal BP, approximate phase I construction context at Structure 4 | Kuijt and Finlayson 2009, pp. 10967–10968 and Figs. 2–5, describe upright notched stones, some reused querns, charred wood and plant impressions in mud. A raised floor for storage is the excavators’ interpretation. Sample ISGS-A0246 is a burned beam associated with the end of phase I, reported as 9,913 ± 59 radiocarbon years BP. The later primary barley study reports grain dimensions consistent with cultivation before domestication. The grain measurements and architecture cannot establish particular harvests or the ownership and contents of the building. |
| Tell Qarassa North, Syria | 10,700–10,200 cal BP, area XYZ-67/68/69, early Pre-Pottery Neolithic B | Arranz-Otaegui et al. 2016, pp. 14003–14004, reports 732 chaff fragments comprising spikelet forks and sections of rachis. Classifiable wild-type or domesticated-type scars occur on 305 fragments, with 427 indeterminate for the trait. Emmer (*Triticum dicoccoides/dicoccum*), einkorn including one- and two-grained forms (*T. boeoticum/monococcum/urartu*) and barley (*Hordeum spontaneum/vulgare*) are represented. The paper reports proportions of domesticated-type fragments from 21.1% to 41.2% across determinations. These are sampled proportions and should not be applied to all cereal plants. The early area XYZ context is supported by eight radiocarbon dates, attributed to Table S3 by the paper. The companion study’s section 6.2 discusses cultivation in phases I–IV around 10.7–10.5 cal ka BP, a narrower context than the whole 2016 study area. |
| Chogha Golan, Iran | About 9,800 cal BP, late archaeological horizon II | Riehl et al. 2013, p. 65, documents the chronological sequence and first reported appearance of domestic-type emmer in the late records of this site. Weide et al. 2015, sections 2.2 and 4–5, compares diagnostic spikelet bases with modern reference plants and reports a *Triticum*-type grain date of 9,831 ± 82 cal BP in horizon II. The contextual event rounds to about 9,800, with no extra confidence interval. Weide et al. 2018, Fig. 3 and Discussion, find domesticated-type emmer chaff in late horizons II and I. They identify substantial earlier gathering of diverse wild grasses and question a simple model of long-term predomestication cultivation. Earlier management of some plants remains a possibility. The late remains establish local use and do not resolve where domestication took place. |

### How the evidence differs

- A food store can hold wild or cultivated food. The floor reconstructed at Dhra′ indicates an architectural solution to keeping food, while the grain study provides a separate argument for early cultivation. A confident inference of a raised floor does not settle what people planted.
- A single rough scar can occur in wild material, including at the base of a brittle barley ear. Fragmentation and charring can alter which parts survive. Grain dimensions, the position of a scar, taxonomic identification and the frequencies of characteristics across a sample all inform interpretation. See Arranz-Otaegui et al. 2016, ‘Cultivation of Wild vs. Domesticated-Type Cereals During the PPNB’, and Weide et al. 2015, introduction and identification key.
- The three stations preserve different kinds of traces and take place in distinct communities. Their order does not imply that a single population progressed from storage to cultivation to domestication.

### Coordinate provenance

- Dhra′, [35.58, 31.25], 1.5 km. Colledge et al. 2018, ‘Dhraʿ: location, excavation and floristic landscape’, opening paragraph. The coordinate locates the site at the scale of an old alluvial terrace.
- Tell Qarassa North, [36.413, 32.833], 1 km. Godon et al. 2015, p. 154, gives 32.832915° N, 36.412547° E for the site complex and distinguishes the two tells. This is a rounded orientation point for the northern tell. The coordinates printed in the 2017 accepted manuscript fall outside the setting it describes and were excluded.
- Chogha Golan, [46.271, 33.377], 500 m. Zeidi and Conard 2013, p. 316, ‘Setting and chronology’, gives 33°22′38.50″ N, 46°16′15.93″ E for the mound by the Konjan Cham River.
