# The Uluburun Shipwreck

Research draft, 2026-11-23. Chapter ID chapter:the-uluburun-shipwreck. Canonical owner package:uluburun-cargo.

The map uses the [Nautical Archaeology Digital Library’s approximate wreck coordinate](https://www.shiplib.org/shipwrecks/uluburun). Pulak's “The Uluburun Shipwreck and Late Bronze Age Trade,” in *Beyond Babylon*, pp. 289–294, anchors the vessel and bulk freight around 1300 BCE. The Atlas renders a rounded comparison window of 1310–1290 BCE. Hull remains and anchors follow Pulak, *INA Quarterly* 26.4, pp. 15–16, Fig. 1. The approximate window covers variant estimates and carries no calendar-year claim.

The copper point is the published locality for Apliki mine. It orients the reader within the regional comparison emphasized by Stos-Gale et al., *Archaeometry* 39, abstract, and Pulak, *Beyond Babylon*, catalogue 185, lead-isotope discussion and notes 23–26. Apliki is one reference locality in the proposed source district. A particular deposit and loading port are unresolved.

Lankton, Pulak and Gratuze, *Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports* 42 (2022), 103354, Abstract and Conclusions, give the compositional support for Egyptian production of the sampled ingots. Amarna Project reports finds from courtyard M50.14–16, including pieces of raw glass, unfinished beads and fireplaces. The Amarna station uses the shared city-level coordinate and period context. Its photograph shows the ship's glass, and the caption gives its find context. The two glass records refer to different collections of material.

The locally included photographs are of ancient finds from Uluburun in the Bodrum Museum. Each asset records the photographer, original source page, licence and image processing. The chapter cites the research publication and excavation sources separately.
