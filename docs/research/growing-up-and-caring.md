# Childhood, support and burial

Research draft restored on 23 November 2026. `chapter:growing-up-and-caring` belongs to `package:early-social-life` and uses the existing `palaeolithic` discovery group. The chapter opens on the editorial viewing window 85,000–40,000 BP. The stops address childhood, support and burial in that order, with the oldest find at the third stop. Each marker locates a distinct site.

| Case | Evidence and atlas treatment | Source locator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| El Sidrón J1 | Daily growth increments in a first molar yield a dental age at death of 7.69 years, range 7.61–7.78. Development of most examined elements is within modern comparative ranges. The atlas and some thoracic vertebrae were less mature. One skeleton informs comparison of individual growth, with population differences still open. | [Rosas et al. 2017, Science 357, 1282–1287](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan6463). [Accepted manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10039466/1/Rosas%20et%20al.%202017.pdf), pp. 4–5, figures 2 and S1–S2, table S3. |
| El Sidrón context | **63,300–43,400 calendar BP**, rounded outwards from the two calibration ranges 59,730–44,570 and 63,282–43,489 BP for contextual sample OxA-21776. The conventional measurement was 48,400 ± 3,200 radiocarbon BP. The atlas uses a broad editorial envelope for the context, with separate qualitative uncertainty and no calendar midpoint. | [Davies et al. 2015, Quaternary Science Reviews 118, 211–242](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2014.12.003), table 6a caption and the OxA-21776 row in the author-posted article. |
| Shanidar 1 | The approximate 50,000 BP marker follows the article's context age. Bone growth narrows both auditory canals, especially the bridged opening on the right. Hearing loss is inferred anatomically. The authors discuss possible social support from combined impairments and survival. Specific helpers and acts of care remain unknown. | [Trinkaus & Villotte 2017, PLOS ONE e0186684](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0186684), Materials and methods final paragraph, Fig. 1, Discussion on Pleistocene human social support. |
| Panga ya Saidi | Homo sapiens child aged approximately 2.5–3.0 years. Pit structure, sediment and anatomical association support deliberate burial in a filled space. The estimated pit infill age is **78,300 ± 4,100 years**. The atlas stores a central estimate and uncertainty bounds of 82,400 and 74,200 BP. The estimate pertains to the burial context, and the bounded instant expresses uncertainty in its date. | [Martinón-Torres et al. 2021, Nature 593, 95–100](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03457-8), especially published pp. 95–98. [Accepted manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10127965/3/Shipton_Martinon-Torres%20et%20al%20Panga%20ya%20Saidi%20Main%20text%20Dec%202020.pdf), sections Context and chronology and Primary and intentional deposit. The chronology follows the published model including sample OSL4. |

The title's „Abschied“ names an accessible theme. The burial features support deliberate placement while beliefs, feelings and the child's original name remain unknown. Mtoto is the research name. Photos are modern site views. The map's BP reference is 1950 and contextual age estimates are rounded approximations.
