# Wiederkommen und bleiben / Places to Return To

23 November 2026. Draft chapter in `postglacial-lifeways`. The 15,000–10,200 BP viewing window spans the selected contexts. A sequence of building activities, the function of a space and the duration of residence are distinct questions.

| Station | Evidence and dating | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shubayqa 1 | Two superimposed hearths in Structure 1. Seven dates on short-lived plant remains from their fills put use around 14,400–14,200 cal BP. Of the 24 examined charred foods, 15 contain cereal tissue. The study compares the texture and inclusions of the pieces with bread-like products. | Dates concern the hearth context. The plant tissue measurements support several possible ingredients, including wild cereals and club-rush storage organs. They give neither a fixed recipe nor the frequency of preparation. |
| Eynan / Ain Mallaha | Records in Layers IV, III and II include Shelters 131, 51 and 62 in one area. Haklay and Gopher's analysis associates a series of surviving postholes with Wall 51. The chapter uses the paper's rounded ~15,000–12,000 cal BP site envelope and places the structures within its earlier part. | The broad envelope describes the site's occupation phases. It gives limited resolution for any particular wall. The proposed connections between posts and walls differ from earlier reconstructions. Later fieldwork and archives support rebuilding and modification over time. |
| Wadi Faynan 16 | Wicks et al. describe the roughly 20 m long O75, plaster surfaces, trough and tiers of benches. Their Table 2 O75 stratigraphic model gives 68.2% estimates of 11,740–11,280 BP for the lower boundary and 10,680–10,260 BP for the upper boundary of activity associated with its use. | These are separate modelled bounds estimated from charcoal contexts, not exact construction or demolition dates. The bounds broaden substantially at 95.4%. The site-wide bird study models seasonal activity. Communal use is an interpretation of the architecture and archaeological context. |

## Source reading

- Arranz-Otaegui, Gonzalez Carretero, Ramsey, Fuller and Richter (2018), PNAS 115, 7925–7930. Main full text read, especially the introduction after Fig. 2, “Identification of Bread-Like Remains,” ingredients and discussion of production. Supplement is referenced by the paper. The chapter uses reported results from the main text. The paper's figures have a CC BY-NC-ND license. [Published full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6077754/).
- Richter, Arranz-Otaegui, Yeomans and Boaretto (2017), Scientific Reports 7, 17025. Existing Atlas source `source:richter-shubayqa-2017` supplies chronology, location and construction context. `place:shubayqa-1`, its geometry, `artifact:shubayqa-structure-1` and `asset:shubayqa-excavation` are reused from `package:deglaciation`. The aerial image is Fig. 1 from the 2017 paper, photo by Alexis Pantos, under CC BY 4.0. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-17096-5).
- Haklay and Gopher (2015), PLOS ONE 10, e0130121. Publisher PDF read, especially pp. 1–3 and 10–13, “The site of Eynan,” “Shelter 131/51,” conclusions and Fig. 9. The updated proposal revisits the groupings of the existing excavated structures and suggests a semicircular building. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0130121).
- Khalaily (2016), Hadashot Arkheologiyot 128, ‘Enot ‘Eynan. The excavation report's first paragraphs give the map reference, site setting and approximate span. [Israel Antiquities Authority report](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/report_detail_eng.aspx?id=24927&mag_id=124).
- The 2025 Eynan reanalysis by the field team was checked through the authors' institutional abstract and indexed sections. The open archive download was blocked. Its summary describes rebuilding and reuse, consistent with the observed stratigraphy. The chapter keeps the broad published site envelope and avoids precise dates from its incompletely accessed table. [Institutional record](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/hunter-gatherer-builders-70-years-of-research-at-the-natufian-ham/).
- Wicks, Finlayson, Maričević, Smith, Jenkins and Mithen (2016), Proceedings of the Prehistoric Society 82, 73–123. Published PDF read for Fig. 4, Table 2, “Structure O75” and conclusions, including old wood, possible redeposition and uncertainty around the site chronology. Its 2015 online date precedes the 2016 issue date. [Published PDF](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/45376/1/S0079497X15000213a.pdf).
- Finlayson, Mithen, Najjar, Jenkins and Smith (2009), Antiquity 83 project gallery. Introduction and Fig. 1 supply the survey grid reference for WF16. [Field report](https://www.antiquity.ac.uk/projgall/finlayson319).
- Mithen et al. (2022), Journal of Quaternary Science, DOI 10.1002/jqs.3429. Abstract and discussion of annual and seasonal activity in the full text. Migratory birds offer evidence for seasons represented at the site. The number and residency of people remain modelled interpretations. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/jqs.3429).

## Map points and figures

Eynan's 2013 report gives easting 253,848–253,975 and northing 776,237–776,374. The midpoint is interpreted in the Israel Transverse Mercator grid (EPSG:2039), transformed to WGS84 and rounded to `[35.573, 33.081]`. The WF16 excavation reference 36R 739824 E 3390442 N is interpreted in WGS84 UTM zone 36N and rounded to `[35.502, 30.623]`. Both receive 1 km editorial allowances, including uncertainty from the source grid interpretation. The chapter reuses the existing Shubayqa point. All are site orientation points.

- `asset:eynan-shelter-51-plan` is the publisher PNG for Fig. 9 of Haklay and Gopher (2015), reproduced at 982 × 1165 pixels. It presents the researchers' proposed plan and cross-section. Original paper credits Haklay and Gopher, under the journal's linked CC BY 4.0 license. The figure has no separate credit or license exception. [Figure](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0130121.g009).
- `asset:wf16-o75-excavation` is the 965 × 552 JPEG embedded in Fig. 4 of Wicks et al. (2016), extracted unchanged. Excavation view in April 2009. The foreground shows the O75 floor as exposed, with Midden O60 beyond it. The paper's CC BY 4.0 statement applies to the figure and the caption gives no separate credit. [Paper with Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1017/ppr.2015.21).

The chapter remains a research draft. The images are local, and their license records identify the respective figures and origins. Chapter membership includes the earlier Structure 1 record while the new hearth event is kept separately dated.
