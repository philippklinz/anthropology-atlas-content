# Darwin and the Beagle, 1831–1836

This chapter covers selected stations of the second Beagle voyage, including Darwin's Galápagos stays. It is not a complete ship's log or a history of evolutionary theory. Sources were consulted by the AI research/implementation assistant on 22 November 2026. Every record remains **draft**; no independent specialist or human review is claimed.

## Scope and reuse

The package owns 67 records: 3 people, the Beagle, 17 places, 20 events (the journey and 19 stations), 18 geometries, 6 sources, the chapter and its package manifest. It reuses Rio Bay and the Cape of Good Hope and their orientation points through `package:maritime-shared`. Participation in the overall journey does not mean continuous presence or a common track. Darwin's Santiago shore camp, while the Beagle sailed elsewhere, is described explicitly.

## Source reading

- Darwin Correspondence Project: voyage introduction, purpose and dates, and the relationship between Darwin and FitzRoy.
- John van Wyhe's Darwin Online itinerary: entries for 1831–1836 and the selected port dates. The short chronology has apparent mistakes (including a Bahia Blanca entry in August 1836); these are not silently adopted.
- Rookmaaker's daily itinerary on Darwin Online: introduction and the Pernambuco section, especially day 1689, 12 August 1836. It independently identifies Recife/Pernambuco. Its date-line comments are not used to invent a modern local-time conversion.
- Grant and Estes's introduction to Darwin's Galápagos geological diary: ship arrival on 15 September, first landing on 16 September, Floreana, Isabela and the 8–17 October Santiago camp, including Covington's participation and the ship's absence.
- Darwin's Beagle diary, Galápagos section: dated island observations. This is a transcription of a historical text, not manuscript consultation.
- Natural History Museum, HMS Beagle bird collection: mockingbirds, Gould's post-voyage classification, FitzRoy's specimens and the later significance of the collections. This supports distinguishing observations on the voyage from later interpretation.

Canonical source records preserve URLs and limitations; records contain locators. No lengthy source text or specimen images are redistributed.

## Dates and geography

Dates follow the consulted voyage chronology. They do not resolve shipboard day-count changes into exact modern local dates. The chapter and voyage cover 27 December 1831 to 2 October 1836. The full journey's Galápagos approach is distinct from Darwin's first landing; the Santiago interval is a land stay, not a stationary ship position.

Points are manually authored, rounded regional orientation aids with unknown historical positional accuracy. Modern country names are geographic orientation, not assertions about nineteenth-century sovereignty. The existing Cape point is expressly a regional marker, not the Beagle's anchorage at the cape itself.

The original route drawing connects selected stations and splits at ±180°. Intermediate vertices shape ocean crossings; they are not observed positions. Stops and repeated coastal trips are omitted. Do not derive sailing distances, speeds, ship locations or archaeological claims from this line. No external vector dataset or image is copied.
