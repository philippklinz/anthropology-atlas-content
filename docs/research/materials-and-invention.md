# Inventing with Stone, Wood and Fibres · research notes

Three independent stations compare evidence for composite tools, worked bone and early clay vessels. All records are research drafts. The shared Palaeolithic discovery facet groups the material for exploration. The viewing window is 70,000–15,000 years BP, with 1950 as the reference year. It is an editorial interval.

### Limits of the reconstructions

Soriano et al. report diterpenes consistent with conifer resin and some lipid material in two Sibudu specimens. The particular extracts yielded no detected polysaccharides. Wadley, Hodgskiss and Grant 2009, ‘Replication Results’ and Table 1, test composite adhesive mixtures. The experiment supports the possibility of adhesives with plant gum and ochre. Chasan et al. 2024, sections 4–5, show why identification to a conifer genus or species can be unreliable after degradation. The chapter does not assign an exact recipe or species to the analysed pieces.

For Fa-Hien Lena the selected images and statements refer to the early assemblage. The possible mesh gauge in Langley et al. 2020, ‘Plant- and/or skin-working technologies’ and Fig. 4A, is a Phase A object. The stated Phase A range is around 6–4 cal ka BP. The 2026 Sumangala et al. publication is noted at abstract level and has a different excavation focus. The window here is explicitly attributed to the 2019/2020 analyses. [Sumangala et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.109985)

### Geometry

The three points are manually derived from the cited geographic facts, with an editorial 200 m tolerance. They orient readers to the sites rather than the exact location of individual finds. Sibudu follows Soriano et al. 2015 (‘Sibudu’, opening paragraph), Fa-Hien Lena follows Wedage et al. 2019 (p. 6, ‘Methods’) and Xianrendong follows Wu et al. 2012 (p. 1697, site description). No route connects the sites.
