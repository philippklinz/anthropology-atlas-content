# Chapter discovery and membership

2026-11-22. All seven chapters remain research drafts.

`recordIds` is an explicit reading scope, independent of canonical package ownership. It includes each chapter’s people, places, events, assertions, geometries and cited sources, plus outgoing references. Shared places retain one ID. Dependencies do not automatically make every shared record a chapter member. The initial lists were seeded from the existing three curatorial packages and checked for reference closure and person counts (4 / 4 / 3).

Discovery summaries and regions summarize the existing cited itineraries. Topics are editorial finding aids, not historical assertions. The European early-modern grouping uses FNZ-Online, “1. Was heißt Frühe Neuzeit?”, paragraphs 1–2, accessed 2026-11-22. Its ca. 1500 boundary includes the transition exemplified by Columbus’s 1492 voyage. The 19th century is explicitly a calendar group (1801–1900), not a universal cultural period. Neither grouping claims to describe periodization in every visited region.

The directory displays only selected chapter members, across all dates. Map time is a separate filter. An entity reused by several chapters appears once with multiple chapter badges.

## Broad early-history grouping

The four early-human chapters reuse `palaeolithic` (Altsteinzeit / Palaeolithic) with the same localized labels and description. This is an editorial finding aid, not a claim of uniform dates or cultural development worldwide. Their separate initial viewing windows remain on their chapter cards. Related additions should reuse suitable existing IDs; a new chapter does not require a new period facet. The schema permits several overlapping groups per chapter when useful.

The explorer uses its period dropdown without a duplicate tile row. Search, regions, topics, dates, multi-selection and chronological chapter order remain available. Branch-specific collections remain a future editorial option, not a new runtime control in this change.
