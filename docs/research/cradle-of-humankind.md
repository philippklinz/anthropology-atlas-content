# Die Wiege der Menschheit / The Cradle of Humankind

Research draft, 2026-11-22. Canonical package: `package:human-origins`; chapter: `chapter:cradle-of-humankind`.

## Scope

A bounded introduction to early Homo sapiens in Africa, using Jebel Irhoud and Omo I as two different kinds of chronological evidence. The 350,000–200,000 BP chapter window is editorial, not the lifespan, origin interval or distribution of a species. It does not claim to cover all human evolution, all contemporary sites, or the history of the South African World Heritage property with a similar name.

The chapter contains 28 members plus its chapter and package: 12 entities (three places, three natural specimens, one taxon, one artifact assemblage, three research/stone-working technologies and one explanatory concept), three occurrences, one dated assertion, three orientation geometries, seven sources and two images. Natural fossils are not classified as manufactured artifacts. Modern dating methods are explanatory concepts without prehistoric invention dates.

## Evidence decisions

| Record | Evidence and locator | Representation |
|---|---|---|
| Jebel Irhoud fossils | Hublin et al. (2017), pp. 289–292 and Figs. 1–3 | Fossil assemblage; early Homo sapiens morphology; no named first human or unique birthplace |
| Layer 7 age | Richter et al. (2017), pp. 293–296 and Methods: thermoluminescence | 315 ± 34 ka, explicitly **1σ**; an occurrence with a date envelope, not a continuous occupation |
| Omo I age | Vidal et al. (2022), pp. 579–583, Figs. 1–2 and dating methods | Explicitly **older than** the correlated 233 ± 22 ka (2σ) eruption; older limit unknown |
| Shala Qi2 eruption | Vidal et al. (2022), same locators | Separate dated occurrence; no reconstructed ash-fall polygon |
| Connected African origins | Hublin et al. (2017); Ragsdale et al. (2023), abstract/discussion | Explanatory concept, not exact routes, named ancient peoples or a direct genealogy for each fossil |

The original Hublin, Richter and Vidal papers were read, including the relevant figures/methods. Ragsdale's accessible full text was consulted for the model's scope. The distinction between 1σ and 2σ is retained; neither is silently converted into a universal hard uncertainty boundary. The atlas uses the reported intervals for filtering and display, without implying zero probability outside them.

The source papers' rounded ages in ka are placed on the atlas's fixed 1950 BP comparison scale. This choice does not claim dating to an exact calendar year, and does not imply radiocarbon calibration. The approximately decades-long reference-year difference is far below the quoted millennial uncertainty, but the convention is disclosed to readers.

## Geography

Three coarse orientation points are manually authored, with editorial accuracy estimates rather than excavation precision:

- Jebel Irhoud: [-8.9, 31.9], estimated 8 km. Based on the coordinates in the publisher's Introduction excerpt for Geraads et al. (2013). Only that location excerpt was accessible; no age/environment claims use that paper.
- Omo I/KHS: [35.9, 5.4], estimated 5 km. McDougall et al. (2005), p. 734, gives 5°24.15′ N, 35°55.81′ E. This older paper supplies location only; the revised age constraint uses Vidal (2022).
- Shala: [38.5, 7.5], estimated 15 km. Approximate caldera orientation from Vidal (2022), Fig. 2. Not a precisely known eruption vent.

These are not ancient territories, settlement polygons, migration tracks or reconstructed palaeocoastlines. The existing physical basemap shows present-day land. Contemporary country labels are orientation aids.
