# Cultivation in Three Landscapes — research draft

23 November 2026. The chapter compares three locally documented cases in the shared *New Ways of Living After the Ice Age* discovery group. Its editorial map window is **10,500–6,400 calendar years BP** (before 1950). The stops follow types of evidence. Their order does not imply a migration or a timeline of plants carried from one place to the next.

## Finds and dates

| Stop | Date used in the atlas | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Xihuatoxtla, northern Guerrero, Mexico | **8,990–8,610 cal BP**, age envelope of layer D charcoal | Ranere et al. 2009, p. 5015, *Results* and Fig. 3, report 7,920 ± 40 radiocarbon BP on charcoal at 65 cm depth. Piperno et al. 2009, pp. 5019–5023, Table 1 and Figs. 1–2, identify maize starch on stone tools and maize cob and squash rind phytoliths in tool or sediment samples. The analysed material includes levels associated with the charcoal date and lower levels. Layer E yielded informative plant traces but no direct age determination in the reported study. The published 8,710 cal BP intercept and conventional summary of about 8,700 calendar BP describe the same context age. The atlas preserves the range. Squash phytoliths support a domesticated form, possibly *Cucurbita argyrosperma*. The species suggestion stays tentative. The date does not belong to a directly measured cob, to an individual grain's harvest, or to the first domestication event. |
| Ñanchoc Valley, CA-09-77, northern Peru | **10,403–10,163 cal BP**, direct AMS date of squash seed Beta 179512 | Dillehay et al. 2007, p. 1890 and Table 1 (published 2σ range), report 9,240 ± 50 radiocarbon BP for the seed from a house floor. The atlas keeps the reported calendar range and records 2σ in the event note, since the native field for sigma requires a central estimate and equal widths on either side. The other dated squash seed, Beta 219589, came from **CA-09-27** (8,535–8,342 cal BP). The article's Fig. 3 is **CA-09-27**, so its photo cannot be labelled as the earliest specimen. Beta 219588 is a younger peanut hull from CA-09-77 (8,640–8,435 cal BP). Beta 183279 is a later cotton specimen from **CA-09-71** (6,278–5,948 cal BP). Neither is assigned the earliest seed's age. The co-authors discuss adoption and early use of cultivated plants while rejecting a demonstrated local origin for their domestication. Other valley evidence includes separate localities and dates. |
| Kuk Swamp, upper Wahgi Valley, Papua New Guinea | **10,220–9,910 cal BP**, Phase 1 channel context. **6,950–6,440 cal BP**, early Phase 2 mounds. | Denham et al. 2003, pp. 190–192, Figs. 2B–C, 3–4 and Table 1. The oldest surface has pits, stakeholes and small channels near a palaeochannel. Its dating and features support possible cultivation, with disagreement about the strength and extent of that inference. Denham, Golson and Hughes 2017, chapter 12, Introduction, documents the disagreement and distinguishes the stronger Phase 2 evidence. Their chapter ties the early subphase to charcoal in basal feature fills and separates a later subphase. Regular mound bases suggest intentionally raised soils. Abundant banana phytoliths in a managed grassland context strengthen the cultivation interpretation. The calendar ranges describe different contexts. Later ditch networks and the earlier sinuous runnel listed under Phase 3 have separate ages. The atlas does not draw continuous fields or place ancient infrastructure on today's landscape. |

The comparison separates processing, the presence and identification of a particular specimen, and archaeological evidence of cultivated ground. It does not project a modern crop suite, a strictly sedentary way of life or an exact point of initial domestication onto these individual sites. Denham et al. argue for independent agricultural development in the New Guinea highlands. Dillehay et al. distinguish crop adoption in Ñanchoc from the origin of those crops. The three comparison points have no connection lines.
