# New Neighbours, New Villages · Research notes

Three local cases fall within the shared editorial grouping `postglacial-lifeways`. The selected viewing interval, 6250–3850 BCE, includes all three cases. Dates retain the calendar BCE convention used by the studies. Internally, 6250 BCE is astronomical year −6249. Present-day country and place names give geographic orientation.

| Evidence | Calendar encoding | Specific source and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lepenski Vir, trapezoidal architecture | Start of I–II 6160–6080 BCE and end 5980–5940 BCE, each with 95% probability in model 1 | Borić 2019, pp. 29 and 43–44, Fig. 11. The interval describes the modelled building phase as a whole. Individual structures can have shorter lives. The same paper discusses the effect of diets rich in aquatic food on skeletal dates and the correction by comparisons with terrestrial material and dietary isotope evidence, pp. 14 and 16–17. [Primary article PDF](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/img/doi/0350-0241/2019/0350-02411969009B.pdf) |
| Five dental enamel outliers in phase I–II | Approximate phase envelope 6200–5950 BCE | Borić and Price 2013, pp. 3298–3302, results for Lepenski Vir and Fig. 2–3. Their transition phase is about 6200–6000/5950 cal BC. The record uses the outer rounded window as a context for the evidence. Five individuals in the paper’s Lepenski Vir sample were assigned to I–II and fell outside the assumed ratio range, as against seven from phase III. The boundary is a regional working estimate and geological overlap limits origins. Teeth preserve a childhood environmental signature, with no exact origin town, route, language or cultural identification recorded. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1211474110) · [full author-posted text](https://www.academia.edu/2553397/Strontium_isotopes_document_greater_human_mobility_at_the_start_of_the_Balkan_Neolithic) |
| Brunn-Wolfholz, early Site 2 | Reported oldest site context c. 5670–5450 cal BCE | Nikitin et al. 2019, Materials and Methods, Brunn 2 archaeological site. Sixteen radiocarbon dates are the basis for the published age envelope of the oldest part. Broader proposals for the multi-area settlement do not supply dates for individual houses. Stadler and Kotova 2011, pp. 152–155 and Fig. 4–6, document long trenches, posthole traces in better-preserved parts of the complex and typical dimensions around 20 × 7–8 m. Some evidence is from other parts of Wolfholz, so details of the best-preserved plans should not be transferred to a specific oldest house at Site 2. [Early context](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-56029-2) · [excavation report](https://www.academia.edu/1898903/) |
| Hornstaad-Hörnle IA, timber and harvest | Site phase 3918–3902 BCE. Burnt layer in 3910 BCE | Styring et al. 2016, Introduction and Hornstaad-Hörnle IA, Fig. 2–3. The article analyses identified cereals from the fire layer and applies Billamboz's published dendrochronology. The construction plan shows dated wood and inferred footprints. Dated posts have different preservation and coverage from the locally sampled burnt layer. Charred wheat and barley were among the crops. Fishing equipment is also attested. [Primary study and plan](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2015.192) |

The geometry records contain three independent site points. Lepenski Vir uses the rounded photograph location [22.317, 44.556]. The 1 km editorial tolerance covers the original excavation and the remains relocated approximately 100 m downstream and 30 m higher, according to p. 44 of *Carpathian Cultural Heritage in Wetlands*. Brunn's [16.2917, 48.1204] rounds the coordinate in Nikitin et al.'s methods and represents the wider complex. The Hornstaad point [9.006, 47.695] rounds UNESCO's component 1363-074, which includes other phases of Hornstaad-Hörnle. [Relocation account](https://www.ramsar.org/sites/default/files/carpathian_cultural_heritage_in_wetlands__0.pdf) · [UNESCO sites and coordinates](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1363/maps/)
