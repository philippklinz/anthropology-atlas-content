# Rice, Millet and Early Pots · research record

Prepared 23 November 2026. Bilingual draft for `postglacial-lifeways` with three case studies. `package:east-asian-lifeways` owns the new site, specimen, artifact, event, assertion, image and source records. The initial window uses the ages of the selected samples, 11,100–6,600 BP.

## Tianluoshan

Fuller et al. use dates on rice grains and nuts to place K3 level 7, layers 8–7, and layers 6–5 around 6,900, 6,750, and 6,600 years BP. The manuscript and supplement describe accelerator radiocarbon measurements, calibration with OxCal 3.10 and Bayesian stratigraphic ordering. The atlas uses the study's rounded phase window. The calibrated dates are distinct from the lab radiocarbon ages and from the wider settlement chronology.

Of 2,727 spikelet bases, 2,641 were classified into wild, immature and domesticated types. The older and younger domestic-type proportions were 27.4% and 38.8% in the pooled phase samples, reported in the chapter as 27% and 39%. The proportion of rice among all sieved plant remains was 8%, 18% and 24%. Figs. 2 and S2 and the associated text provide these comparisons. The sampling supplement explains that K3 is the intact midden context, whereas other contexts include pit fills and dispersed domestic remains. Preservation and deposition affect the observed plant counts. The co-occurrence of potential field weeds and changing attachment scars supports cultivation and selection, with continued use of wild resources. The evidence cannot give exact shares of human food intake or a single first date of domestication. [Fuller et al. (2009), pp. 1607–1610 and Supplementary Information](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/108026/)

The chapter's graphic uses four published numbers and a new layout. It is an original illustration of the counts with distinct denominators. The archive says its manuscript is for personal use and restricts redistribution. No article figure is bundled.

## Cishan

The main article describes degraded fills in loess-cut pits, husk phytolith comparison and radiocarbon measurements. Lu et al. assign 27 samples from CS-I, CS-III and CS-IV to c. 10,300–8,700 cal BP and describe their diagnostic structures as broomcorn millet. The younger group from CS-II, CS-V and the Cishan Museum sample BWG is predominantly broomcorn millet by their phytolith classification, with a small share of forms attributed to foxtail millet. The molecular analyses in Fig. 5 include BWG and CS-V-03, both from the younger group. The biomarker result is kept separate from the early-group chronology. [Lu et al. (2009), pp. 7367–7372, Results and Figs. 1–5](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0900158106)

Zhao notes that the early dated material likely came from naturally exposed pit fills and that the material was likely bulk samples rather than securely identified individual grains. The earlier archaeological excavations produced younger dates. The museum sample CS-BWG from those excavations, dated by Lu et al. and discussed by Zhao on p. S301, gave 7,671–7,596 cal BP. The atlas keeps that sample's reported range as uncertain bounds without supplying a midpoint or statistical confidence level. The proposed 10,300–8,700 cal BP context window carries a disputed status and no claim about the earliest agriculture. The chapter distinguishes plant identification, date assignment and domestication. The 2009 article's full main text and Fig. 3 caption were consulted. Table S1 was not used independently because the publisher supplement link returned the main page. [Zhao (2011), p. S301, The Origin of Dry-Land Agriculture in North China](https://doi.org/10.1086/659308)

## Torihama

Use the site's own case rather than the generic dates for the archipelago. The bounds 11,100–8,000 cal BP refer to the Initial Jōmon sample group in Table 1. The full study spans intermittent occupations from the Late Pleistocene to the mid-Holocene and reports 143 vessels. It includes earlier and younger groups. The paper states that the Initial Jōmon charred surface deposits yielded the full suite of aquatic biomarkers in this study, while analysed Initial Jōmon vessels lacking such deposits did not give this signal. The strong freshwater signal in compound-specific carbon isotopes in combination with aquatic markers belongs to the younger Early Jōmon group, 7.9–5.1 ka cal BP in Table 1. The reading text mentions it as a later comparison. It does not change the mapped Initial Jōmon window. The plant and animal remains at the site attest foods that are rarely visible in the tested pots. Some processing signals remain ambiguous. The study's main text and Table 1 were consulted in the publisher PDF indexed by web retrieval. The supplemented individual-sherd data were not required for this chapter and are not cited as read. [Lucquin et al. (2016), Results, Table 1, Fig. 3 and Discussion](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/113/15/3991.full.pdf)
