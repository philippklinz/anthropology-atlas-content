# Ein Tag zum Überleben / A Day of Making a Living

Research draft, 23 November 2026. The chapter compares plant fragments, food and processing tools, and diet inferred from chemistry. Three geographic points mark different contexts. The chapter window of 71,000–13,000 BP is editorial. It gives space around the dated finds. All dates in the catalogue use the 1950 reference year.

| Station | Interpretation | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Madjedbebe | Florin et al. associate charred non-wood plant remains in Phase 2 with human food use and processing. Phase 2 is approximately 65,000–53,000 years old in their chronology. | The underlying sediment age and association with artifacts are debated. Preservation and charring shape the sample. The count cannot be converted into dietary proportions. |
| Ohalo II | Snir et al. describe wild cereals, a grinding slab, and a blade with wear consistent with cutting plants. Zohar et al. examine approximately 17,000 fish remains. The site window is 23,500–22,500 cal BP. | The window includes repeated occupations. It gives neither the lifespan of one blade nor uninterrupted residence. The authors' trial cultivation hypothesis is separate from domestication. The blade preserves cutting edge and hafting traces, while the full handle is missing. |
| Taforalt | Moubtahij et al. integrate enamel, dentin, bone and amino acid isotope results. They infer a substantial plant component in the sampled people's diets. The directly dated burial envelope in the study is 15,077–13,892 cal BP. | The graph includes trophic position and isotope signals, rather than calorie shares. The conclusion concerns sampled people and allows animal food. The ranges give the dated context, rather than the duration of one diet. |
