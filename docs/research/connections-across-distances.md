# Connected Across Long Distances · research record

Draft chapter in the shared Palaeolithic discovery group. The 50,000–14,000 BP viewing window includes three stops: an east/south comparison, one strontium analysis in Lesotho, and manufacture in the southern Kalahari. Four events mark archaeological evidence at findsites.

## Evidence and chronology

**Comparative measurements.** Miller and Wang's study compares 1,516 beads from 31 sites. In the published data the 50–33 ka bin contains 14 southern beads, 12 from Border Cave. The authors interpret cross-regional similarities in size as evidence of social connections. This comparison is sensitive to the concentration of samples. The roughly 3,000 km span is a regional comparison. Direct contact between the two particular sites and any particular itinerary remain undetermined.

Selected rows of the released measurement CSV give 43,448 calibrated/approximate BP for Enkapune ya Muto DBL1.4 entries including #5 and #9, and 42,538 for Border Cave R24 Level 30″ – 1WA (1BS LR). The atlas rounds to 43,400 and 42,500. These contextual estimates lack a supplied confidence range for the individual rows. The 50–33 ka bin is a study period. The two display points date their selected contexts. [Miller & Wang (2022), Regional and chronological bead metrics, Discussion and Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04227-2) · [Authors’ measurement CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alsjmonsoon/Ostrich-egg-shell-bead-data/main/OstrichEggShellBeads_Miller%26Wang.csv)

**Material provenance.** Stewart et al. Table 1 gives 33.7–31.9 calibrated ka BP for Sehonghong I13-170. Its strontium ratio is 0.718649 ± 0.000013 (two standard errors). The age bounds and analytical uncertainty describe different measurements. The geological baseline and ecological assumptions about ostrich habitat support a nonlocal origin. For three beads including I13-170, the study calculates a minimum of 326 km from the midpoint between Sehonghong and Melikane. The exact egg-laying location, the sequence of movement and possible handovers remain undetermined. Table 1 assigns the analysed Melikane beads to the late Holocene, 3.3–0.15 ka. [Stewart et al. (2020), Table 1, Discussion and Fig. 6](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/brianstewart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1097/2023/06/Stewart_etal_2020PNAS.pdf)

**Manufacture.** Hatton et al. identify 19 beads, nine preforms and 21 other shell fragments in the DBGS sediment at Ga-Mohana Hill North. Nearly every manufacturing stage is represented. The quartz OSL context age is 14.8 ± 0.8 ka. The atlas places it approximately on the 1950 BP axis, with bounds 15,600–14,000 BP and a central estimate of 14,800. A possible offset from the measurement convention is small relative to the dating uncertainty. No statistical confidence level is assigned to the bounds. The one event shows age uncertainty of the context. It does not encode the duration of manufacture. The regional MIS 2 comparison gives additional evidence of bead use. Ga-Mohana itself is younger than 19 ka. [Hatton et al. (2022), Ga-Mohana Hill North Rockshelter, Results, Fig. 2 and Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0268943)
